10-Year Outcomes of Initial CTO-PCI vs No CTO-PCI Strategies for Chronic Total Occlusion: The DECISION-CTO Randomized Trial.

Kim JH et al. · PubMed

In the 10-year extended follow-up of DECISION-CTO, an initial CTO-PCI strategy did not reduce the composite of death, myocardial infarction, stroke or any revascularization compared with an initial no CTO-PCI strategy, although wide confidence intervals leave both benefit and harm possible.

Design
Open-label, multicenter randomized trial (19 centers in 5 Asian countries) with extended 10-year follow-up; modified intention-to-treat analysis.
Population
834 patients with chronic total occlusion randomly assigned between March 2010 and October 2016; 804 included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis (413 CTO-PCI, 391 no CTO-PCI). Clinical follow-up at 10 years was complete in 688 patients (85.6%), without a significant between-group difference (P = 0.69).
Intervention
Initial strategy of percutaneous coronary intervention of the chronic total occlusion, with guideline-directed medical therapy and PCI of obstructive non-CTO lesions encouraged.
Comparison
Initial strategy of no CTO-PCI, with guideline-directed medical therapy and PCI of obstructive non-CTO lesions encouraged in both groups.
Outcomes
Primary endpoint was the composite of death from any cause, myocardial infarction, stroke, or any revascularization at 10 years: 134 patients in the CTO-PCI group versus 138 in the no CTO-PCI group (Kaplan-Meier estimates 33.9% vs 37.4%; risk difference -3.5 percentage points; 95% CI -10.4 to 3.3 percentage points; P = 0.32).

Other results

  • Primary composite endpoint at 10 years: 33.9% (CTO-PCI) vs 37.4% (no CTO-PCI); risk difference -3.5 percentage points (95% CI -10.4 to 3.3); P = 0.32.
  • Restricted mean event-free survival time at 10 years: 7.52 vs 7.53 years; difference -0.01 years (95% CI -0.54 to 0.52); P = 0.97.
  • Death from any cause: 12.7% vs 16.5%; HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.52-1.12); P = 0.16.
  • Follow-up completeness at 10 years was 85.6% (688 of 804 patients), with no significant between-group difference (P = 0.69).

Strengths

  • Randomized allocation with an exceptionally long 10-year follow-up horizon for a CTO strategy trial, and high follow-up completeness (85.6%).
  • Both arms received guideline-directed medical therapy and PCI of non-CTO lesions, isolating the effect of treating the occlusion itself.
  • Analysis included a restricted mean event-free survival time estimate, which is less dependent on proportional hazards than a hazard ratio over a decade of follow-up.

Limitations

  • The confidence interval for the primary risk difference (-10.4 to 3.3 percentage points) is wide, so a clinically meaningful benefit or harm cannot be excluded; the authors state this precludes definitive conclusions.
  • Open-label design, and any revascularization is part of the composite primary endpoint, which can be influenced by unblinded clinicians and patients.
  • The original trial enrolled fewer patients than planned relative to its design assumptions and the extended analysis is not a fresh randomization, so the 10-year comparison is underpowered for hard endpoints such as mortality.
  • Conducted entirely at Asian centers between 2010 and 2016, so generalizability to contemporary CTO technique, equipment and other populations is uncertain.
  • Crossover and the modified intention-to-treat analysis set (804 of 834 randomized) may dilute the comparison; funding source not reported in the abstract.

Practice implications

Ten-year data continue to provide no evidence that opening a chronic total occlusion as an initial strategy improves hard outcomes over medical therapy with non-CTO revascularization, supporting symptom relief rather than event reduction as the main rationale for CTO-PCI; the imprecision of the estimate means the question is not definitively settled.

Circulation 2026-11-16 RCT Cardiac surgery n = 323 impact 4/5

Biatrial Versus Left Atrial Maze Ablation During Cardiac Valve Surgery for Persistent Atrial Fibrillation: The ABLATION Trial.

Li Z et al. · PubMed

In patients with persistent or long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation undergoing rheumatic mitral valve surgery, a complete biatrial Cox-Maze IV lesion set gave higher 12-month freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias than a left atrial lesion set (78.7% vs 68.6%; adjusted difference 10.7%) without an excess of permanent pacemakers.

Design
Multicenter, randomized, single-blind superiority trial with a prespecified noninferiority assessment for pacemaker implantation; 1:1 allocation; intention-to-treat analysis. Surgeons were trained and certified before the trial and all procedures were videorecorded and centrally reviewed.
Population
323 randomized patients with persistent or long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation undergoing rheumatic mitral valve surgery, with or without concomitant cardiac surgical procedures. Mean age 56.5 years, 72.1% women, mean left atrial diameter 51.9 mm. All 323 were included in the intention-to-treat analysis (164 biatrial, 159 left atrial).
Intervention
Biatrial maze ablation: a complete Cox-Maze IV procedure.
Comparison
Left atrial maze ablation: a left-sided maze lesion set.
Outcomes
Primary outcome was freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias lasting >30 seconds without antiarrhythmic drugs within 12 months: 78.7% (129 of 164) with biatrial versus 68.6% (109 of 159) with left atrial ablation (adjusted difference 10.7% [1-sided lower 95% CI, 3%]; P=0.01). Key secondary endpoint was freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation: 97.0% (159 of 164) versus 98.1% (156 of 159), meeting the predefined noninferiority margin of -5% (adjusted difference -1.1% [1-sided lower 95% CI, -3.9%]; Pnoninferiority=0.01).

Other results

  • Freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias >30 seconds without antiarrhythmic drugs at 12 months: 78.7% (129/164) biatrial vs 68.6% (109/159) left atrial; adjusted difference 10.7% (1-sided lower 95% CI, 3%); P=0.01.
  • Freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation: 97.0% (159/164) biatrial vs 98.1% (156/159) left atrial; adjusted difference -1.1% (1-sided lower 95% CI, -3.9%); Pnoninferiority=0.01 against a -5% margin.
  • Baseline substrate was advanced: mean left atrial diameter 51.9 mm, mean age 56.5 years, 72.1% women.

Strengths

  • Randomized, multicenter design directly addressing the lesion-set question that guidelines leave open, with a clinically meaningful rhythm endpoint off antiarrhythmic drugs.
  • Strong procedural quality control: pretrial surgeon training and certification, with all procedures videorecorded and centrally reviewed, reducing variability in how each lesion set was delivered.
  • Single-blind design with complete intention-to-treat inclusion of all 323 randomized patients.
  • The clinically relevant trade-off (pacemaker requirement) was prespecified as a key secondary endpoint with a formal noninferiority margin rather than reported only descriptively.

Limitations

  • Enrolled only patients undergoing rheumatic mitral valve surgery, with a young, predominantly female population and markedly dilated atria, so results may not transfer to degenerative valve disease or to coronary or aortic surgery.
  • Follow-up is limited to 12 months, and durability of the biatrial advantage and late pacemaker requirement beyond one year are not reported.
  • The absolute pacemaker difference was assessed against a -5% margin in a trial of 323 patients, so an uncommon but real excess of conduction injury cannot be excluded.
  • Rhythm monitoring intensity and method for detecting episodes >30 seconds are not described in the abstract, and detection method strongly influences recurrence rates.
  • Surgeons could not be blinded to the lesion set; mortality, stroke, cross-clamp and operative times, and other safety outcomes are not reported in the abstract, nor is the funding source.

Practice implications

For patients with persistent AF and a dilated left atrium undergoing rheumatic mitral valve surgery, this supports performing the full biatrial Cox-Maze IV rather than a left-sided-only lesion set, since the right atrial lesions added roughly 10 percentage points of rhythm success without a demonstrable pacemaker penalty, provided the operator is trained in the complete procedure.

J Am Coll Cardiol 2026-11-01 RCT Interventional n = 1,053 impact 4/5

Angiography-Derived vs Wire-Based Fractional Flow Reserve to Guide Revascularization: The Randomized LIPSIA-STRATEGY Trial.

Majunke N et al. · PubMed

Wire-free angiography-derived vessel FFR met the prespecified absolute noninferiority margin against pressure-wire FFR for guiding revascularization of intermediate stenoses at 1 year (3.0% vs 4.1% major adverse cardiac events), but the event rate was lower than assumed, and on a relative margin noninferiority was not met in the primary analysis.

Design
Multicenter, investigator-initiated, randomized 1:1 noninferiority trial at 8 German centers; primary analysis by modified intention to treat against an absolute noninferiority margin of 3.5 percentage points.
Population
1,053 patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes and at least 1 intermediate coronary stenosis, randomized between June 2020 and February 2025; median age 69 years (vFFR n = 528, FFR n = 525). The primary outcome was ascertainable in 986 of the 1,053 randomized patients.
Intervention
Revascularization guided by angiography-derived vessel fractional flow reserve (vFFR), without a pressure wire.
Comparison
Revascularization guided by pressure-wire fractional flow reserve (FFR).
Outcomes
Primary outcome was major adverse cardiac events (composite of cardiac death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or unplanned clinically driven revascularization) within 1 year: 15 of 493 patients (3.0%) with vFFR versus 20 of 493 (4.1%) with FFR; risk difference -1.01 percentage points; P for noninferiority <0.001. The upper boundary of the 1-sided 97.5% CI for the risk difference was 1.29 percentage points, below the prespecified 3.5 percentage point margin (relative risk 0.75; 95% CI 0.39-1.45).

Other results

  • Major adverse cardiac events at 1 year: 3.0% (15/493) vFFR vs 4.1% (20/493) FFR; risk difference -1.01 percentage points; P for noninferiority <0.001.
  • Upper boundary of the 1-sided 97.5% CI for the risk difference 1.29 percentage points, below the prespecified margin of 3.5 percentage points; relative risk 0.75 (95% CI 0.39-1.45).
  • Physiological values of 0.80 or less occurred in 43.1% (vFFR) vs 35.9% (FFR) of patients.
  • The study lesion was revascularized in 41.2% (vFFR) vs 35.5% (FFR) of patients.
  • In a post hoc sensitivity analysis using the relative margin of 1.44 implied by the original assumptions, noninferiority was not met in the primary modified intention-to-treat population, but was met in the corresponding post hoc per-protocol analysis.

Strengths

  • Randomized head-to-head comparison of two guidance strategies with a clinical composite endpoint rather than agreement or diagnostic accuracy alone, which is what prior inconsistent studies largely offered.
  • Multicenter, investigator-initiated design enrolling both acute and chronic coronary syndromes, reflecting the mixed population in which intermediate lesions are actually assessed.
  • Reasonable size for a strategy trial (1,053 randomized) with high outcome ascertainment (986 of 1,053) and a prespecified noninferiority framework.
  • The authors transparently report that the absolute margin became less conservative on a relative scale than intended and show the discordant post hoc sensitivity analyses rather than only the favorable one.

Limitations

  • The observed 1-year event rate was far lower than assumed, so the 3.5 percentage point absolute margin corresponds to a large relative difference; on the implied relative margin of 1.44 noninferiority was not met in the primary modified intention-to-treat population.
  • The event count is small (15 and 20 events), making the comparison imprecise (relative risk 0.75; 95% CI 0.39-1.45) and underpowered to detect a clinically relevant difference in hard endpoints.
  • vFFR guidance classified more patients as physiologically significant (43.1% vs 35.9%) and led to more study-lesion revascularization (41.2% vs 35.5%); as the authors note, whether that additional revascularization is beneficial is not answered by similar 1-year event rates.
  • One year of follow-up is short for a strategy that defers or performs PCI; divergence could emerge later.
  • Open-label strategy allocation, with unplanned clinically driven revascularization as part of the primary composite, leaves room for ascertainment bias; conducted at 8 German centers, so operator expertise with vFFR may not generalize. Funding source not reported in the abstract.
  • The per-protocol analysis met the relative margin while the modified intention-to-treat analysis did not, a discordance that favors caution rather than reassurance.

Practice implications

Angiography-derived vFFR is a credible wire-free option for assessing intermediate stenoses and may increase uptake of physiology-guided decisions, but this trial does not establish equivalence robustly: it classified more lesions as significant and triggered more PCI, and its support for noninferiority depends on which margin is applied. Pressure-wire FFR remains the reference standard where a definitive physiological answer matters.

Acute pulmonary embolism: long-term cause-specific mortality in a Chinese nationwide registry.

Cheng CY et al. · PubMed

In a Chinese nationwide registry of 66,192 patients hospitalised with acute pulmonary embolism, 5-year cumulative all-cause mortality was 22.41%, with nearly half of deaths from non-cardiovascular causes and ischaemic heart disease the largest named cause beyond 12 months, arguing that follow-up after PE should look well beyond the thrombotic event itself.

Design
Registry-based, consecutive-enrolment national cohort study (3,223 Chest Pain Centres across China, 2018-2023; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04014972). Mortality ascertained by national registry linkage; cumulative incidence functions for temporal mortality patterns and Fine-Gray competing-risk models for exploratory associations with cause-specific mortality.
Population
66,192 patients consecutively enrolled with acute pulmonary embolism from 3,223 Chest Pain Centres across China between 2018 and 2023.
Intervention
Not an intervention study; the exposure of interest was hospitalisation with acute pulmonary embolism, with exploratory examination of age, sex, region (Western China residence) and vital signs.
Comparison
No comparator cohort; comparisons were internal, between causes of death, time intervals since the index event, sexes and age strata.
Outcomes
Cumulative all-cause and cause-specific mortality to 60 months. At 60 months, cumulative all-cause mortality was 22.41% (95% CI 21.92-22.91), comprising cardiovascular mortality 11.84% (95% CI 11.46-12.23) and non-cardiovascular mortality 10.57% (95% CI 10.22-10.92).

Other results

  • Cumulative all-cause mortality at 60 months: 22.41% (95% CI 21.92-22.91).
  • Cardiovascular mortality 11.84% (95% CI 11.46-12.23); non-cardiovascular mortality 10.57% (95% CI 10.22-10.92).
  • Causes of death varied over time: acute PE and ischaemic heart disease predominated initially, malignancy peaked at 1-3 months, and ischaemic heart disease was the largest named cause in each interval beyond 12 months.
  • Among cardiovascular deaths, ischaemic heart disease was the predominant cause (35.7%); among non-cardiovascular deaths, malignancy (43.3%) and chronic pulmonary disease (24.6%) predominated.
  • Men had higher cumulative cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular mortality than women, while women had a higher proportion of cardiovascular causes among deaths.
  • All-cause mortality rose steeply with age, from 11.52% at <55 years to 38.51% at >=75 years; acute PE and malignancy dominated in younger patients while cardiovascular and pulmonary disease prevailed in older ones.
  • Exploratory Fine-Gray analyses identified age, Western China residence and vital signs as factors associated with mortality.

Strengths

  • Very large, consecutively enrolled national cohort (66,192 patients from 3,223 centres), giving precise cumulative incidence estimates with narrow confidence intervals.
  • Mortality ascertained through national registry linkage rather than site reporting, limiting loss to follow-up and outcome misclassification for the fact of death.
  • Appropriate use of competing-risk methods (cumulative incidence functions and Fine-Gray models) for cause-specific mortality, which avoids the overestimation that Kaplan-Meier analysis would produce.
  • Reports mortality by interval since the index event rather than as a single endpoint, which is what makes the shifting cause-of-death pattern visible and clinically actionable.

Limitations

  • Observational registry design with no comparator population, so the excess mortality attributable to pulmonary embolism itself, as distinct from underlying comorbidity such as cancer, cannot be quantified.
  • Enrolment via Chest Pain Centres in China may select more severe or more acutely presenting cases and limits generalisability to other health systems and to outpatient-managed pulmonary embolism.
  • Cause of death from registry linkage is prone to misclassification, particularly in distinguishing fatal pulmonary embolism from ischaemic heart disease or sudden death; attribution of 'largest named cause' depends on local coding practice.
  • Analyses of associated factors are explicitly exploratory; no adjusted effect estimates, confidence intervals or P values for these factors are given in the abstract, and multiplicity across causes, intervals and subgroups is not addressed.
  • Treatment received, anticoagulation duration, PE severity classification, cancer status at baseline and recurrence are not reported in the abstract, so the drivers of the observed mortality cannot be assessed; median follow-up duration and funding source are also not reported.
  • With enrolment through 2023 and 60-month estimates, the longest-term figures rest on the earliest enrolled subset rather than the full cohort.

Practice implications

Survivors of acute pulmonary embolism carry a roughly one-in-five risk of death over 5 years, and most of that risk is not from recurrent thromboembolism: ischaemic heart disease and malignancy dominate after the first months. Follow-up should therefore pair decisions about anticoagulation duration with deliberate cardiovascular risk assessment and attention to occult cancer in the early period, with intensity adapted to age. The data support evaluating risk-adapted long-term follow-up strategies rather than defining one.
