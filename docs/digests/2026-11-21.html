10-Year Outcomes of Initial CTO-PCI vs No CTO-PCI Strategies for Chronic Total Occlusion: The DECISION-CTO Randomized Trial.

Kim JH et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: The long-term clinical effects of percutaneous coronary intervention for chronic total occlusion (CTO-PCI) remain uncertain. OBJECTIVES: This study aimed to compare 10-year clinical outcomes between initial CTO-PCI and no CTO-PCI strategies in the extended follow-up of the DECISION-CTO trial. METHODS: The DECISION-CTO trial was an open-label, multicenter, randomized trial conducted at 19 centers in 5 Asian countries. Patients were randomly assigned to an initial strategy of CTO-PCI or no CTO-PCI, with guideline-directed medical therapy in both groups and PCI of obstructive non-CTO lesions encouraged in both groups. The primary endpoint was a composite of death from any cause, myocardial infarction, stroke, or any revascularization at 10 years, analyzed in the modified intention-to-treat group. RESULTS: Between March 2010 and October 2016, 834 patients were randomly assigned, and 804 of these patients were included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis (413 CTO-PCI, 391 no CTO-PCI). Clinical follow-up at 10 years was complete in 688 patients (85.6%), without a significant between-group difference (P = 0.69). At 10 years, the primary endpoint had occurred in 134 patients in the CTO-PCI group and 138 patients in the no CTO-PCI group (Kaplan-Meier estimates: 33.9% vs 37.4%; risk difference: -3.5 percentage points; 95% CI: -10.4 to 3.3 percentage points; P = 0.32). Restricted mean event-free survival time at 10 years was also similar between groups (7.52 vs 7.53 years; difference: -0.01 years; 95% CI: -0.54 to 0.52 years; P = 0.97). Death from any cause did not differ significantly between groups (12.7% vs 16.5%; HR: 0.76; 95% CI: 0.52-1.12; P = 0.16). CONCLUSIONS: Over 10 years, an initial strategy of CTO-PCI was not associated with a significant difference in the primary endpoint or all-cause mortality compared with an initial strategy of no CTO-PCI. However, the wide CIs preclude definitive conclusions regarding the potential benefit or harm of an initial CTO-PCI strategy. (Drug-Eluting Stent Implantation Versus Optimal Medical Treatment in Patients With Chronic Total Occlusion [DECISION-CTO]; NCT01078051; extended follow-up, NCT06908499).

Circulation 2026-11-16 RCT Cardiac surgery n = 323 impact 4/5

Biatrial Versus Left Atrial Maze Ablation During Cardiac Valve Surgery for Persistent Atrial Fibrillation: The ABLATION Trial.

Li Z et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Guidelines recommend concomitant surgical ablation during cardiac surgery in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF). However, the optimal lesion set remains uncertain, with ongoing debate regarding the relative benefits and risks of biatrial versus left atrial (LA) maze ablation. METHODS: In this multicenter, randomized, single-blind trial, patients with persistent or long-standing persistent AF undergoing rheumatic mitral valve surgery, with or without concomitant cardiac surgical procedures, were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to undergo biatrial or LA maze ablation. The biatrial group underwent a complete Cox-Maze IV procedure; the LA group received a left-sided maze lesion set. To ensure standardization and consistency of the ablation procedures, surgeons underwent pretrial training and certification, and all procedures were videorecorded and centrally reviewed. The primary outcome was freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmias lasting >30 seconds without antiarrhythmic drugs within 12 months. The key secondary end point was freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation. RESULTS: All 323 randomized patients were included in the intention-to-treat analysis (mean age, 56.5 years; 72.1% women; mean LA diameter, 51.9 mm). At 12 months, 78.7% of patients (129 of 164) in the biatrial group and 68.6% (109 of 159) in the LA group were free from atrial tachyarrhythmias (adjusted difference, 10.7% [1-sided lower 95% CI, 3%]; P=0.01). Freedom from permanent pacemaker implantation was noted in 97.0% (159 of 164) of the biatrial group and 98.1% (156 of 159) of the LA group, meeting the predefined noninferiority criterion with a margin of -5% (adjusted difference, -1.1% [1-sided lower 95% CI, -3.9%]; Pnoninferiority=0.01). CONCLUSIONS: Among patients with persistent or long-standing persistent AF undergoing rheumatic cardiac valve surgery, biatrial maze ablation was superior to LA maze ablation in reducing recurrent atrial tachyarrhythmias, while maintaining noninferiority with respect to permanent pacemaker implantation. These findings provide direct evidence to inform lesion-set selection for surgical ablation during cardiac surgery. REGISTRATION: URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT05021601.

J Am Coll Cardiol 2026-11-01 RCT Interventional n = 1,053 impact 4/5

Angiography-Derived vs Wire-Based Fractional Flow Reserve to Guide Revascularization: The Randomized LIPSIA-STRATEGY Trial.

Majunke N et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Wire-based physiological assessment is recommended to guide coronary revascularization but remains underused. Angiography-derived physiology offers a wire-free alternative; however, randomized comparisons with pressure wire fractional flow reserve (FFR) have yielded inconsistent findings. OBJECTIVES: This study sought to determine whether angiography-derived vessel fractional flow reserve (vFFR)-guided revascularization is noninferior to pressure wire FFR guidance for intermediate coronary stenoses. METHODS: The multicenter, investigator-initiated, randomized LIPSIA-STRATEGY trial included patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes and at least 1 intermediate coronary stenosis at 8 German centers. Patients were randomized 1:1 to undergo revascularization based on vFFR or FFR. The primary outcome was major adverse cardiac events, defined as a composite of cardiac death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or unplanned clinically driven revascularization within 1 year, analyzed by modified intention to treat against a noninferiority margin of 3.5 percentage points. RESULTS: Between June 2020 and February 2025, 1,053 patients (median age: 69 years) underwent randomization (vFFR, n = 528; FFR, n = 525). At 1 year, major adverse cardiac events occurred in 15 of 493 patients (3.0%) in the vFFR group and in 20 of 493 (4.1%) in the FFR group (risk difference: -1.01 percentage points; P for noninferiority <0.001); the primary outcome was ascertainable in 986 of the 1,053 randomized patients. The upper boundary of the 1-sided 97.5% CI for the risk difference was 1.29 percentage points, below the prespecified margin (relative risk: 0.75; 95% CI: 0.39-1.45). Physiological values of 0.80 or less occurred in 43.1% vs 35.9% of patients, and the study lesion was revascularized in 41.2% vs 35.5%, respectively. CONCLUSIONS: In patients with intermediate coronary stenoses requiring physiological assessment, a vFFR-guided revascularization strategy met the prespecified absolute criterion for noninferiority to wire-based FFR at 1 year. Because the observed event rate was lower than anticipated, the margin was less conservative on a relative scale than intended. In a post hoc sensitivity analysis using the relative margin of 1.44 implied by the original assumptions, noninferiority was not met in the primary modified intention-to-treat population. In the corresponding post hoc per-protocol sensitivity analysis, noninferiority was met using this relative margin. Whether that additional revascularization is beneficial is not answered by similar 1-year event rates. (Comparison of Vessel-FFR Versus FFR in Intermediate Coronary Stenoses; NCT03497637; Protocol ID HRC045277.).

Eur Heart J 2026-11-20 Observational Thrombosis and vascular n = 66,192 impact 4/5

Acute pulmonary embolism: long-term cause-specific mortality in a Chinese nationwide registry.

Cheng CY et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Long-term cause-specific mortality after acute pulmonary embolism (PE) remains poorly characterized. This study investigated cumulative mortality, temporal changes in causes of death, and associated factors in a national registry. METHODS: This registry-based cohort study consecutively enrolled 66 192 acute PE patients from 3223 Chest Pain Centres across China (2018-2023) (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04014972). Mortality was ascertained through national registry linkage. Cumulative incidence functions estimated temporal mortality patterns, while Fine-Gray models exploratorily examined factors associated with cause-specific mortality. RESULTS: At 60 months, cumulative all-cause mortality was 22.41% (95% confidence interval [CI] 21.92-22.91), comprising cardiovascular (CV) (11.84%, 95% CI 11.46-12.23) and non-CV mortality (10.57%, 95% CI 10.22-10.92). Causes of death varied over time: acute PE and ischaemic heart disease (IHD) predominated initially, malignancy peaked at 1-3 months, and IHD was the largest named cause in each interval beyond 12 months. Among CV deaths, IHD was the predominant cause (35.7%); malignancy (43.3%) and chronic pulmonary disease (24.6%) predominated among non-CV deaths. Men had higher cumulative CV and non-CV mortality than women; while women only had a higher proportion of CV causes among deaths. All-cause mortality increased with age (from 11.52% at <55 to 38.51% at ≥75 years); acute PE/malignancy dominated in younger patients, while CV/pulmonary disease prevailed in older ones. Exploratory analyses identified age, Western China residence, and vital signs as factors associated with mortality. CONCLUSIONS: Among patients hospitalised with acute PE, 5-year cumulative mortality was 22.4%, and nearly half of deaths were from non-CV causes. Shifting mortality patterns highlight the need to evaluate risk-adapted long-term follow-up strategies.
