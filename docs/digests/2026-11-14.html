Intensive Blood-Pressure Control in Patients with Previous Ischemic Stroke.

Sampaio Silva G et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Whether intensive blood-pressure control reduces cardiovascular risk after cerebrovascular events remains uncertain. METHODS: In this multicenter, open-label, randomized trial conducted at 30 sites in Brazil, we assigned participants with elevated blood pressure and previous ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack to receive intensive treatment targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 120 mm Hg or to receive standard treatment targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 140 mm Hg. The primary outcome was a composite of death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization for unstable angina or heart failure. RESULTS: A total of 4368 participants were enrolled, with 2172 assigned to intensive treatment and 2196 to standard treatment. The mean (±SD) age of the participants was 63.9±11.2 years, and 2093 (47.9%) were women. During the follow-up period, excluding the first 3 months, the mean systolic blood pressure was 122.0±11.5 mm Hg (median, 119.6 mm Hg) in the intensive-treatment group and 135.2±9.7 mm Hg (median, 135.0 mm Hg) in the standard-treatment group, with a between-group mean difference of 13.1 mm Hg (95% confidence interval [CI], 12.5 to 13.8). Over a median follow-up of 3.9 years, a primary-outcome event occurred in 236 participants (10.9%; 3.25 events per 100 person-years) with intensive treatment and in 297 (13.5%; 4.11 events per 100 person-years) with standard treatment (hazard ratio, 0.79; 95% CI, 0.67 to 0.94; P = 0.007). The composite renal outcome (sustained reduction of ≥50% in the estimated glomerular filtration rate or progression to end-stage renal disease) was more common with intensive treatment. The overall incidence of serious adverse events was similar in the two groups. CONCLUSIONS: Among participants with previous ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack, targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 120 mm Hg resulted in a lower incidence of major cardiovascular events than targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 140 mm Hg. (Funded by Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein and the Brazilian Ministry of Health; OPTIMAL-STROKE ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT04036409.).

N Engl J Med 2026-11-07 RCT Prevention and lipids n = 8,322 impact 5/5

Pelacarsen and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with High Lipoprotein(a).

Cho L et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Pelacarsen, an antisense oligonucleotide, reduces serum levels of lipoprotein(a). However, the effect of pelacarsen on cardiovascular outcomes remains uncertain. METHODS: We conducted an international, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving patients with preexisting cardiovascular disease and a lipoprotein(a) level of at least 70 mg per deciliter. Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive subcutaneous pelacarsen (80 mg) administered monthly or matching placebo. The primary end point was a major adverse cardiovascular event, defined as a composite of death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or urgent coronary revascularization. RESULTS: A total of 8350 patients underwent randomization, with 8322 included in the efficacy analysis; 4157 patients were assigned to receive pelacarsen, and 4165 to receive placebo. The mean age of the patients was 59.7 years, and 27.0% were women. The median lipoprotein(a) level at baseline was 236 nmol per liter. At 12 months, the lipoprotein(a) level was 64.4 nmol per liter in the pelacarsen group and 233.4 nmol per liter in the placebo group. The median follow-up was 4.7 years. A primary end-point event occurred in 511 patients (12.3%) in the pelacarsen group and in 503 (12.1%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.02, 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.90 to 1.15, P = 0.76). Among patients with a baseline lipoprotein(a) level of at least 90 mg per deciliter, a primary end-point event occurred in 402 of 3266 patients (12.3%) in the pelacarsen group and in 403 of 3274 (12.3%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 1.00; 95% CI, 0.87 to 1.15; P = 0.99). Serious adverse events occurred in 1553 of 4166 patients (37.3%) who received pelacarsen and in 1709 of 4154 (41.1%) who received placebo. CONCLUSIONS: Pelacarsen did not result in a lower risk of major adverse cardiovascular events than placebo at a median follow-up of 4.7 years. (Funded by Novartis; Lp[a]HORIZON ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT04023552.)See also in NEJM Evidence: Extended Analyses of Pelacarsen and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with High Lipoprotein(a).

N Engl J Med 2026-11-08 RCT Electrophysiology n = 685 impact 5/5

Left Bundle-Branch Pacing versus Right Ventricular Pacing for Atrioventricular Block.

Qian Z et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Right ventricular pacing is associated with cardiac dyssynchrony and left ventricular systolic dysfunction. Left bundle-branch pacing preserves left ventricular electrical and mechanical synchrony, but its clinical benefits relative to right ventricular pacing in patients with atrioventricular block remain unclear. METHODS: In this investigator-initiated, international, open-label, randomized, controlled trial, we assigned patients with atrioventricular block in a 1:1 ratio to receive left bundle-branch pacing or right ventricular pacing. The primary outcome was a composite of death from any cause, hospitalization for heart failure, or pacing-induced cardiomyopathy. RESULTS: A total of 702 patients underwent randomization, with 348 assigned to receive left bundle-branch pacing and 354 to receive right ventricular pacing; 17 patients (6 in the left bundle-branch pacing group and 11 in the right ventricular pacing group) were excluded before device implantation. A total of 685 patients (342 in the left bundle-branch pacing group and 343 in the right ventricular pacing group) were included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis. The mean age of the patients was 68.4 years, and 57.5% were men. At the prespecified interim analysis, the data monitoring committee recommended early termination of the trial. At a median follow-up of 35.1 months (interquartile range, 23.6 to 40.7), a primary-outcome event had occurred in 16 patients in the left bundle-branch pacing group and in 35 patients in the right ventricular pacing group (Kaplan-Meier 4-year cumulative incidence, 8.5% and 19.2%, respectively; hazard ratio, 0.41; 95% confidence interval, 0.23 to 0.75; P = 0.003 by a stratified log-rank test). Serious adverse events unrelated to implantation procedures occurred in 39 patients (11.8%) who had received left bundle-branch pacing and in 33 patients (9.3%) who had received right ventricular pacing (P = 0.32). CONCLUSIONS: Among patients with atrioventricular block, left bundle-branch pacing was superior to right ventricular pacing in reducing the risk of a composite of death from any cause, hospitalization for heart failure, or pacing-induced cardiomyopathy. (Funded by Project on New Technology of Jiangsu Province and others; OptimPacing ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT04624763.).

N Engl J Med 2026-11-12 RCT Thrombosis and vascular n = 15,317 impact 4/5

Prevention of Venous Thromboembolism after Total Hip or Knee Replacement.

Pellegrini VD et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Studies comparing aspirin with anticoagulants for venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis after total hip or knee arthroplasty have had conflicting results. METHODS: In a comparative-effectiveness noninferiority trial, we randomly assigned patients undergoing elective hip or knee arthroplasty to receive aspirin (81 mg twice daily), warfarin (to a target international normalized ratio of 2.0), or rivaroxaban (10 mg daily) for 30 days. The composite primary effectiveness outcome (death from any cause or symptomatic VTE) and primary safety outcome (bleeding, reoperation for wound problems, or myocardial infarction) were evaluated through 6 months in a blinded fashion. The noninferiority margin for the risk difference was 0.69 percentage points. RESULTS: Among 18,883 participants who had undergone randomization, 15,317 (7214 with hip arthroplasty and 8103 with knee arthroplasty) were included in the primary per-protocol analysis. A primary effectiveness outcome event occurred in 77 of 6090 participants (1.26%) in the aspirin group, in 52 of 4231 (1.23%) in the warfarin group, and in 47 of 4996 (0.94%) in the rivaroxaban group; a primary safety outcome event occurred in 177 (2.91%), 142 (3.36%), and 174 (3.48%), respectively. In pairwise comparisons, 6-month risks of a primary effectiveness outcome event were 1.24% and 0.94% for aspirin versus rivaroxaban (difference, 0.30 percentage points; 95% confidence interval [CI], -0.20 to 0.81); 1.24% and 1.01% for aspirin versus warfarin (difference, 0.23 percentage points; 95% CI, -0.34 to 0.81); and 1.13% and 1.10% for warfarin versus rivaroxaban (difference, 0.03 percentage points; 95% CI, -0.57 to 0.63). Risks of a primary safety outcome event were 3.70% and 3.02% for rivaroxaban versus aspirin (difference, 0.68 percentage points; 95% CI, -0.04 to 1.41); 3.65% and 2.88% for warfarin versus aspirin (difference, 0.77 percentage points; 95% CI, -0.04 to 1.58); and 3.59% and 3.61% for rivaroxaban versus warfarin (difference, -0.02 percentage points; 95% CI, -0.89 to 0.85). Intention-to-treat analyses yielded similar results. CONCLUSIONS: Aspirin prophylaxis after total hip or knee arthroplasty was not noninferior to rivaroxaban or warfarin with respect to death or symptomatic VTE. Warfarin and rivaroxaban were not noninferior to aspirin for safety. (Funded by the Patient-Centered Outcomes Research Institute; PEPPER ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT02810704.).

N Engl J Med 2026-11-08 RCT Acute and critical care n = 22,459 impact 4/5

Community and EMS Interventions to Improve Survival in Cardiac Arrest.

Malta Hansen C et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Early cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and defibrillation improve survival after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, but evidence from randomized trials about whether community-based implementation strategies improve outcomes is lacking. METHODS: We conducted a registry-based, cluster-randomized trial involving adults with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest treated by emergency medical services (EMS) in North Carolina counties. The counties were grouped into clusters, which were then randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to implement a multifaceted intervention or administer usual care. The intervention included four components: training of 911 telecommunicators to improve rapid recognition of cardiac arrest, training of 911 telecommunicators to improve delivery of CPR instructions from telecommunicators to bystanders, community CPR training, and enhanced use of automated external defibrillators by first responders or bystanders before EMS arrival. Data were collected through the Cardiac Arrest Registry to Enhance Survival. The primary outcome was survival to hospital discharge with a good neurologic outcome (Cerebral Performance Category score of 1 or 2; range, 1 to 5, with higher scores indicating worse neurologic outcome). RESULTS: From July 1, 2022, to June 30, 2026, a total of 28 clusters comprising 11,615 patients were assigned to the intervention group, and 27 clusters comprising 10,844 patients were assigned to the usual-care group. The median age of the patients was 64.0 years, and 36.7% were women. The percentage of patients who survived to hospital discharge with a good neurologic outcome was 9.0% (1048 of 11,608 patients) in the intervention group and 9.4% (1017 of 10,839 patients) in the usual-care group (adjusted odds ratio, 0.97; 95% confidence interval, 0.75 to 1.26; P = 0.80). CONCLUSIONS: Among adults with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, implementation of a multifaceted strategy involving the community and EMS did not result in a greater percentage of patients who survived to hospital discharge with a favorable neurologic outcome than usual care. (Funded by the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute of the National Institutes of Health; RACE-CARS ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT04660526.).

Effect of discontinuing antihypertensives on perioperative hypotension in older patients before non-cardiac surgery: multicentre, randomised, double blind, controlled trial.

Yang Y et al. · PubMed

Abstract

OBJECTIVE: To determine whether discontinuing versus continuing angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) on the morning of surgery reduces perioperative hypotension in older patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery. DESIGN: Multicentre, randomised, double blind, placebo controlled trial. SETTING: Five tertiary teaching hospitals in China, November 2020 to December 2023. PARTICIPANTS: 2036 older patients (60-80 years) using ACE inhibitors or ARBs for at least two weeks and scheduled for non-cardiac surgery under general anaesthesia. INTERVENTIONS: Patients were randomised to receive their usual ACE inhibitor or ARB or a placebo in identical capsules on the morning of surgery. MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: The primary outcome was the incidence of perioperative hypotension (mean blood pressure <65 mm Hg or a ≥30% decrease from baseline requiring treatment). Secondary outcomes included duration of hypotension, requirement for a vasopressor, intraoperative fluid volume, postoperative hypotension, and 30 day complications (neurocognitive disorders, major cardiocerebral events) or mortality. RESULTS: 2012 patients (mean age 68.8 years; 54.4% male) completed the study: 1004 were included in the antihypertensive group and 1008 in the no antihypertensive group. The incidence of perioperative hypotension was lower in the no antihypertensive group (314/1008, 31.2%) versus antihypertensive group (457/1004, 45.5%): adjusted odds ratio 0.52 (95% confidence interval (CI) 0.43 to 0.63); absolute risk difference -14.4% (95% CI -18.5% to -10.1%); P<0.001. The duration of hypotension and requirement for a vasopressor were both reduced in the no antihypertensive group: adjusted mean difference -1.3 minutes (95% CI -1.8 to -0.8 minutes) and 28.5% v 41.1%; adjusted odds ratio 0.54 (95% CI 0.44 to 0.66). Intraoperative fluid volume, postoperative hypotension, 30 day complications, and mortality did not differ significantly between groups. CONCLUSIONS: Discontinuing ACE inhibitors or ARBs on the morning of surgery in older patients with hypertension statistically significantly reduced perioperative hypotension and the requirement for vasopressors without increasing 30 day complications or mortality. This simple strategy provides high certainty evidence for preoperative drug management in this patient population. TRIAL REGISTRATION: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2000039376.

Cardiovascular outcomes with survodutide in adults with obesity at increased cardiovascular risk (SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT): a double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial.

Bozkurt B et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Survodutide, a glucagon receptor and GLP-1 receptor dual agonist, improved markers of cardiometabolic health in people with obesity, but cardiovascular safety for this new class remains to be established. METHODS: SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT was a global, phase 3, randomised, double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled, multicentre, event-driven, cardiovascular safety trial conducted at 524 clinical sites in 34 countries. We included participants aged 18 years or older with a BMI of 27 kg/m2 or greater and established cardiovascular disease or at least two obesity complications or risk factors for cardiovascular disease; participants with chronic kidney disease were eligible if they also had a BMI of 30 kg/m2 or greater. Participants were randomly assigned by interactive response technology (1:1:1) to once-weekly subcutaneous survodutide (up-titrated to 3·6 mg or 6·0 mg) or placebo. The primary endpoint was a composite of death from cardiovascular causes, non-fatal stroke, non-fatal myocardial infarction, ischaemia-related coronary revascularisation, or heart failure event (targeting 189 primary endpoint events). We assessed the non-inferiority of pooled survodutide compared with placebo with a margin of 1·65 for the upper limit of the two-sided 95% CI of the hazard ratio (HR). Efficacy and safety analyses included all randomly assigned participants who received at least one dose of survodutide or placebo. The primary analysis was performed according to the treatment-regimen estimand, which assessed treatment effects regardless of any early discontinuation of survodutide or placebo, temporary stop, the use of protocol-prohibited obesity medications, or a prolonged dose-escalation period. This completed trial is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06077864. FINDINGS: From Nov 20, 2023, to Nov 8, 2024, we screened 7753 participants, of whom 5530 (71·3%) participants were recruited and randomly assigned to treatment. 23 (0·4%) participants were not treated; 1836 (33·3%) of 5507 participants received survodutide 3·6 mg, 1835 (33·3%) received 6·0 mg, and 1836 (33·3%) received placebo; 2194 (39·8%) were female, 3313 (60·2%) were male, and 4332 (78·7%) were White. Median time in study was 22·0 months (IQR 20·1-23·7). 1029 (56·0%) in the survodutide 3·6 mg group, 1039 (56·6%) in the survodutide 6·0 mg group, and 1083 (59·0%) in the placebo group remained on treatment until study closeout. The primary endpoint occurred in 136 (3·7%) of 3671 participants in the survodutide group and in 76 (4·1%) 1836 participants in the placebo group (HR 0·89 [95% CI 0·67-1·18; p<0·0001 for non-inferiority). Adverse events occurred in 1708 (93·0%) of 1836 participants with survodutide 3·6 mg, 1708 (93·1%) of 1835 with survodutide 6·0 mg, and 1571 (85·6%) of 1836 with placebo. Gastrointestinal adverse events were more frequent with survodutide than in the placebo group (nausea occurred in 882 [48·0%] of 1836 participants in the 3·6 mg group and 900 [49·0%] of 1835 in the 6·0 mg group vs 223 [12·1%] of 1836 in the placebo group, and vomiting occurred in 582 [31·7%] participants in the 3·6 mg group and 623 [34·0%] in the 6·0 mg group vs 89 [4·8%] in the placebo group). Gastrointestinal adverse events leading to treatment discontinuations occurred in 354 (19·3%) participants in the survodutide 3·6 mg group, 381 (20·8%) in the survodutide 6·0 mg, and 34 (1·9%) in the placebo group. Serious adverse events occurred at similar rates across treatment groups (413 [22·5%] in the survodutide 3·6 mg group, 381 [20·8%] in the survodutide 6·0 mg, and 439 [23·9%] in the placebo group). Fatal adverse events were similar across treatment groups (16 [0·9%] in the survodutide 3·6 mg group, 16 [0·9%] in the survodutide 6·0 mg, and 11 [0·6%] in the placebo group) and were primarily due to cardiac disorders (six [0·3%] in the survodutide 3·6 mg group, seven [0·4%] in the survodutide 6·0 mg, and six [0·3%] in the placebo group). INTERPRETATION: In participants with obesity with established cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease or at increased risk of cardiovascular disease, survodutide was not associated with increased risk for the primary composite endpoint of death from cardiovascular causes, non-fatal stroke, non-fatal myocardial infarction, ischaemia-related coronary revascularisation, or heart failure event compared with placebo. This cardiovascular safety trial provides clinicians with reassuring data that treatment with survodutide, a novel glucagon receptor-GLP-1 receptor dual agonist, is not associated with increased cardiovascular risk. FUNDING: Boehringer Ingelheim.

Interleukin-6 Inhibition with Ziltivekimab for Atherosclerotic Disease.

Ridker PM et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: The inflammatory cytokine interleukin-6 is hypothesized to be a therapeutic target for patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney disease. Whether neutralizing interleukin-6 levels with ziltivekimab, a monoclonal antibody, reduces the risk of adverse cardiovascular events remains unknown. METHODS: We conducted a multinational, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving adults with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and cardiovascular inflammation (defined by a high-sensitivity C-reactive protein [CRP] level of ≥2 mg per liter). Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive subcutaneous ziltivekimab (15 mg once monthly) or placebo. The primary efficacy end point was a 3-point major adverse cardiovascular event (MACE). This composite end point included death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke. RESULTS: A total of 6376 patients were included in the full analysis population: 3184 were assigned to receive ziltivekimab and 3192 to receive placebo. The mean age of the patients was 69.4 years, and 27.5% were women. At 24 months, the between-group difference in the median percent change in the levels of inflammatory biomarkers among patients receiving ziltivekimab as compared with those receiving placebo was -76 percentage points for free interleukin-6 and -64 percentage points for high-sensitivity CRP. During a median follow-up of 33 months, a primary end-point event occurred in 561 patients (17.6%) in the ziltivekimab group and in 565 patients (17.7%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.99; 95% confidence interval, 0.88 to 1.11; P = 0.84). Serious adverse events occurred in 54.6% of the patients in the ziltivekimab group and in 54.8% of those in the placebo group; serious infections occurred in 20.4% and 17.9%, respectively. CONCLUSIONS: Among patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and cardiovascular inflammation, a strategy of lowering interleukin-6 levels with ziltivekimab did not result in a lower risk of cardiovascular events than placebo. (Funded by Novo Nordisk; ZEUS ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT05021835.).

Discontinuing Statins in Older Adults without Cardiovascular Disease.

Rodondi N et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Whether discontinuing statin therapy in older adults with at least two chronic conditions but no known cardiovascular disease affects the time to a first cardiovascular event is not well characterized. METHODS: We conducted an international, open-label, randomized, controlled trial that enrolled adults at least 70 years of age who were taking statins for primary prevention. Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either discontinue or continue statin therapy and other lipid-lowering therapies. The primary outcome was a composite of nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal ischemic stroke, or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. The primary analysis assessed noninferiority on the basis of the between-group difference in restricted mean event-free survival time at 2 years, with a noninferiority margin of -5.2 weeks. RESULTS: A total of 1880 participants underwent randomization: 938 were assigned to the discontinuation group and 942 to the continuation group. The mean age of the participants was 77.5 years, and 46.9% were women. The between-group difference in the restricted mean event-free survival time at 2 years was -2.0 weeks (95% confidence interval, -3.7 to -0.3; P<0.001 for noninferiority). Over a median follow-up of 103 weeks, 119 participants (12.7%) in the discontinuation group and 87 (9.2%) in the continuation group had a primary-outcome event. At least one serious adverse event occurred in 43.0% of the participants in the discontinuation group and in 39.8% in the continuation group. CONCLUSIONS: Among older adults with at least two chronic conditions but no known cardiovascular disease, discontinuation of statin therapy was noninferior to continuation with respect to major cardiovascular events or death from any cause at 2 years. (Funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation; STREAM ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT05178420.).

Intensive Blood-Pressure Lowering in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes.

Santo K et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Uncertainty persists regarding the effects of intensive blood-pressure lowering in patients with type 2 diabetes. METHODS: In this multicenter, open-label, randomized trial conducted in Brazil, patients with elevated blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, and high cardiovascular risk received intensive therapy targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 120 mm Hg or standard therapy targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 140 mm Hg. Cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor in acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI): a randomised, double-blind trial.

Motovska Z et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock (AMI-CS) can compromise the administration and absorption of oral P2Y12 inhibitors and carries high ischaemic and mortality risks. This trial assessed whether intravenous cangrelor was non-inferior to crushed oral ticagrelor for short-term clinical outcomes and whether it provided faster and more consistent platelet inhibition in patients with AMI-CS. METHODS: DAPT-SHOCK-AMI was a double-blind trial conducted at 29 hospitals across five European countries (Czech Republic, France, Germany, Poland, and Slovakia). Patients older than 18 years with AMI-CS were randomly assigned (1:1 ratio, stratified by centre, age, sex, presence or absence of ST-segment elevation, and mechanical ventilation) to cangrelor (30 μg/kg bolus followed by an infusion of 4 μg/kg per min for at least 2 h) or crushed ticagrelor (180 mg loading dose). The primary laboratory endpoint was a platelet reactivity index of less than 50% at the end of primary percutaneous coronary intervention. The primary clinical endpoint was death, myocardial infarction, or ischaemic stroke at 30 days. The primary clinical endpoint was analysed for non-inferiority with a prespecified margin of 1 percentage point among all randomly assigned patients who received study treatment. The study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03551964) and EudraCT (2018-002161-19); recruitment and 1-year follow-up are complete, and 5-year follow-up is ongoing. FINDINGS: Between Aug 1, 2018, and Jan 19, 2024, 605 patients (137 [23%] were female) were randomly assigned to cangrelor (n=298) or ticagrelor (n=307); no surviving patients were lost to the 1-year follow-up. The primary laboratory endpoint was analysed in 56 (19%) patients in the cangrelor group and 68 (22%) in the ticagrelor group, and was achieved in 56 (100%) of 56 patients receiving cangrelor and 15 (22%) of 68 receiving ticagrelor (p<0·0001). The 30-day primary clinical endpoint occurred in 112 (38%) of 298 patients receiving cangrelor and 126 (41%) of 307 receiving ticagrelor; non-inferiority was not demonstrated (difference -4 percentage points [90% CI -10 to 3]; Pnon-inferiority=0·13). Major bleeding at 30 days occurred in 19 (6%) of 298 patients receiving cangrelor and 16 (5%) of 307 receiving ticagrelor. INTERPRETATION: To our knowledge, this is the first randomised trial to compare P2Y12 inhibitor strategies specifically in patients with AMI-CS. Cangrelor achieved effective platelet inhibition more frequently than crushed ticagrelor at the end of primary percutaneous coronary intervention. Non-inferiority of cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor was not demonstrated for the primary 30-day clinical endpoint. These trial findings can inform future trials of P2Y12 inhibition strategies in patients with AMI-CS. Statin-Ezetimibe Combination Therapy versus Statin Monotherapy in Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease: A Prespecified Secondary Analysis of the Randomized Ez-PAVE Trial.

Lee SH et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: In a target-directed low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C)-lowering strategy, the impact of the initial treatment regimen has not been investigated in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. We compared the efficacy and safety of initial statin-ezetimibe combination therapy versus statin monotherapy within intensive or conventional LDL-C-targeting strategies. METHODS: In this prespecified second randomization of the randomized Ez-PAVE trial (Effects of Ezetimibe Combination Therapy for Patients With Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease: Randomized Comparison of LDL Cholesterol Targeting <70 versus <55 mg/dL) conducted at 17 centers in South Korea, a total of 3048 patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease were enrolled between January 2021 and July 2022. Following the first randomization to either an intensive (<55 mg/dL) or conventional (<70 mg/dL) LDL-C-targeting strategy, patients underwent subsequent randomization (initial statin-ezetimibe combination therapy [n=1525] versus statin monotherapy [n=1523]) and were followed up for 3 years. Lipid-lowering therapy was adjusted according to a predefined protocol to achieve LDL-C targets. The primary end point was a 3-year composite of death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, any revascularization, or hospitalization for unstable angina. RESULTS: The mean±SD age was 64.4±9.0 years, and 638 patients (20.9%) were female. The incidence of the primary end point did not differ between statin-ezetimibe combination therapy (128 patients [8.5%]) and statin monotherapy (119 patients [7.9%]; hazard ratio, 1.07 [95% CI, 0.83-1.37]; P=0.596). Abstract

BACKGROUND: Percutaneous left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) is increasingly used as an alternative to oral anticoagulation for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation. OBJECTIVES: We evaluated the efficacy and safety of LAAC vs contemporary medical therapy in a high-risk population using individual participant data from randomized trials. METHODS: Bibliographic databases and trial registries through August 5, 2026, were searched for randomized trials comparing percutaneous LAAC with contemporary medical therapy in populations with a trial-level mean CHA2DS2-VASc score ≥4. Both primary outcomes were time to first stroke, systemic embolism, or cardiovascular/unknown death (efficacy) and time to first major bleeding (safety). A flexible time-varying Cox model was used for major bleeding. The protocol was submitted to PROSPERO (CRD420261335972). RESULTS: CLOSURE-AF (Left Atrial Appendage CLOSURE in Patients With Atrial Fibrillation Compared to Medical Therapy) and PRAGUE-17 (Left Atrial Appendage Closure vs. Novel Anticoagulation Agents in Atrial Fibrillation) contributed 1,290 participants (647 assigned to LAAC and 643 to control). Median age was 77.3 years (Q1-Q3: 71.4-81.9 years), and 809 participants (62.7%) were men. The primary efficacy outcome occurred in 142 LAAC participants (21.9%) and 133 control subjects (20.7%) (adjusted HR: 0.98; 95% CI: 0.77-1.24; P = 0.842). Major bleeding occurred in 86 LAAC participants (13.3%) and 80 control subjects (12.4%) (adjusted HR: 1.03; 95% CI: 0.75-1.39; P = 0.874). The treatment effect on major bleeding varied over time (P for treatment-by-time interaction < 0.001), with a cumulative major bleeding incidence of 9.4% with LAAC vs 6.0% with control (absolute risk difference, 3.4 percentage points; 95% CI: 0.4-6.3 percentage points) at 1 year and 13.2% vs 12.1% (absolute risk difference, 1.1 percentage points; 95% CI: -2.8 to 5.0 percentage points) at 3 years. The major bleeding hazard varied over time, with higher early and lower later conditional hazard estimates; however, a cumulative bleeding benefit was not demonstrated by 3 years. Long-term randomized data are needed to improve the precision of later treatment-effect estimates.
