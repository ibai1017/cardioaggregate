Randomized trials

JAMA 2026-10-28 RCT Electrophysiology n = 482 impact 4/5

Left Bundle-Branch Area Pacing vs Right Ventricular Pacing for Atrioventricular Block and Preserved Left Ventricular Function: The LEAP-Block Randomized Clinical Trial.

Li X et al. · PubMed

In patients with atrioventricular block, preserved ejection fraction and an anticipated pacing burden above 40%, left bundle-branch area pacing cut the 24-month composite of death, heart failure events or upgrade to biventricular pacing from 12.5% to 2.4% (absolute risk difference -10.1%; 95% CI, -16.8% to -3.4%; P = .003), driven by fewer heart failure events rather than by mortality.

Design
Prospective, multicenter, randomized, controlled, superiority trial (LEAP-Block) at 14 Chinese hospitals, January 2021 to August 2025, with last follow-up on June 3, 2025; 1:1 randomization and 24-month follow-up. Masking not reported.
Population
499 patients with left ventricular ejection fraction 50% or greater and atrioventricular block requiring ventricular pacing with an anticipated pacing burden greater than 40%, randomized from 551 assessed for eligibility; the primary analysis included 482 patients (mean age, 67 years; 46% female).
Intervention
Left bundle-branch area pacing (LBBAP), n = 249 randomized and 242 analysed.
Comparison
Conventional right ventricular pacing (RVP), n = 250 randomized and 240 analysed.
Outcomes
Primary end point, the 24-month composite of all-cause death, heart failure events or upgrade to biventricular pacing: 2.4% with LBBAP versus 12.5% with RVP (absolute risk difference, -10.1% [95% CI, -16.8% to -3.4%]; P = .003). The 24-month Kaplan-Meier estimated cumulative incidence of the primary end point was 5.0% for LBBAP versus 13.2% for RVP (hazard ratio, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.19 to 0.73]; P = .004). Secondary end points included heart failure events, 3.4% versus 11.3% (absolute risk difference, -7.9% [95% CI, -13.0% to -2.8%]); no significant differences were observed in all-cause death, upgrade to biventricular pacing, device-related complications or pacing-induced cardiomyopathy (individual event counts not reported).

Other results

  • Primary composite end point at 24 months: 2.4% (LBBAP) versus 12.5% (RVP); absolute risk difference, -10.1% (95% CI, -16.8% to -3.4%); P = .003.
  • Kaplan-Meier cumulative incidence of the primary end point at 24 months: 5.0% versus 13.2%; hazard ratio, 0.37 (95% CI, 0.19 to 0.73); P = .004.
  • Heart failure events: 3.4% versus 11.3%; absolute risk difference, -7.9% (95% CI, -13.0% to -2.8%).
  • All-cause death, upgrade to biventricular pacing, device-related complications and pacing-induced cardiomyopathy did not differ significantly between groups.
  • Acute procedural success was similar: 237 of 242 (97.9%) with LBBAP versus 239 of 240 (99.6%) with RVP; P = .22.
  • Left bundle-branch capture was confirmed in only 188 of 236 patients (79.7%) assigned to LBBAP.
  • Superiority of LBBAP was consistent across all prespecified subgroups.
  • Registered as NCT04730921; funding not reported.

Strengths

  • Randomized, multicenter, prespecified superiority design with clinical end points, addressing a pacing-mode question that has until now rested largely on observational and physiological data.
  • Enrolled exactly the population in which the question matters most: preserved ejection fraction with an anticipated high pacing burden (greater than 40%), where pacing-induced cardiomyopathy is the main hazard.
  • Follow-up of 24 months with a clinically meaningful composite that includes death and heart failure events rather than only electrocardiographic or echocardiographic surrogates.
  • Acute procedural success was high and nearly identical in the two arms, so the outcome difference is unlikely to reflect a failure to deliver the assigned therapy.
  • Treatment effect was consistent across all prespecified subgroups, and the effect size is large (hazard ratio, 0.37).

Limitations

  • Conducted entirely at 14 Chinese hospitals by operators experienced in conduction system pacing, so procedural success and complication rates may not transfer to lower-volume centres.
  • Masking is not reported; heart failure events and the decision to upgrade to biventricular pacing are both susceptible to assessor and operator bias in an unblinded pacing trial.
  • The composite is driven by heart failure events (3.4% versus 11.3%); there was no significant difference in all-cause death, and the trial is far too small to address mortality.
  • Event numbers are small in absolute terms, so the confidence interval around the hazard ratio is wide (0.19 to 0.73) and the point estimate is likely to be imprecise.
  • 17 of 499 randomized patients were not in the primary analysis (482 analysed), so the analysis is not strictly intention to treat.
  • Left bundle-branch capture was confirmed in only 79.7% of the LBBAP arm, meaning roughly a fifth received deep septal rather than true conduction system capture; the trial therefore tests an anatomical strategy rather than confirmed conduction system pacing.
  • The crude proportions (2.4% versus 12.5%) and the Kaplan-Meier estimates (5.0% versus 13.2%) differ appreciably, implying censoring or incomplete follow-up that the abstract does not quantify.
  • 24 months may be short relative to the time course over which pacing-induced cardiomyopathy and lead-related problems accrue, and long-term LBBAP lead performance is not addressed.
  • Funding and sponsor role are not reported.

Practice implications

This is the first randomized outcome evidence that pacing mode matters clinically in atrioventricular block with preserved ejection fraction, and it supports offering left bundle-branch area pacing rather than right ventricular pacing to patients expected to be paced more than 40% of the time, where operator experience allows. The benefit shown is prevention of heart failure events, not of death, and the modest size, single-country setting, unreported masking and 79.7% rate of confirmed left bundle-branch capture mean it strengthens rather than settles the case; confirmation in a larger multinational trial with longer follow-up and lead-performance data would still be valuable.

Lancet 2026-10-29 Meta-analysis Acute and critical care n = 110,933 impact 4/5

Performance of 0/1 h versus 0/3 h pathways in patients with suspected myocardial infarction: a systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.

Boeddinghaus J et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: In patients with suspected myocardial infarction, the 0/1 h pathway (blood draws at 0 h and 1 h) is recommended in current guidelines for early risk stratification. However, individual trials used different safety outcomes, and efficacy outcomes were inconclusive or not assessed. We conducted a systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis of all randomised controlled trials to synthesise the totality of the evidence, assess heterogeneity across trials, and evaluate clinically relevant outcomes using harmonised definitions across trials. METHODS: In this systematic review and individual participant data meta-analysis, we searched PubMed, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Scopus, and the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform from inception to July 6, 2026, without language restrictions, for randomised trials comparing the safety and efficacy of 0/1 h versus 0/3 h pathways (blood draws at 0 h and 3 h, ie, standard care). Individual participant data were obtained and outcome definitions were harmonised. The primary analysis used a two-stage random-effects meta-analysis. After receipt of the individual participant data but before data harmonisation or analysis, the primary safety outcome was refined to a composite of all-cause death or new type 1 myocardial infarction within 30 days, from the originally specified composite of all-cause death or any new myocardial infarction. The primary efficacy outcomes were emergency department length of stay and direct discharge from the emergency department. This meta-analysis is registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261383452). FINDINGS: Our systematic review identified five randomised trials, all of which provided individual participant data. Among these, the definition of the primary safety outcome varied, and in four trials emergency department length of stay was not reported for the whole analysis population. We included 110 933 patient presentations (55 489 managed with the 0/1 h pathway and 55 444 managed with the 0/3 h pathway). Within 30 days, all-cause death or new type 1 myocardial infarction occurred in 446 patient presentations (0·8%) with the 0/1 h pathway versus 562 patient presentations (1·0%) with the 0/3 h pathway (random-effects pooled odds ratio [OR] 0·83 [95% CI 0·45-1·53]; I2=62·9%; τ2=0·143). Emergency department length of stay was 314 min (IQR 211-481) in the 0/1 h pathway group versus 308 min (209-453) in the 0/3 h pathway group (random-effects pooled geometric mean ratio 1·03 [95% CI 0·85-1·25]; I2=99·2%; τ2=0·024). Direct discharge from the emergency department occurred in 37 190 patient presentations (67·0%) with the 0/1 h pathway versus 36 485 patient presentations (65·8%) with the 0/3 h pathway (random-effects pooled OR 1·31 [95% CI 0·79-2·16]; I2=97·8%; τ2=0·156). INTERPRETATION: Across the available randomised trials, short-term adverse event rates were low with both pathways, but the pooled safety estimate remained imprecise. On average, earlier cardiac troponin retesting did not reduce emergency department length of stay or clearly increase direct discharge, and substantial between-study heterogeneity indicates that treatment effects might not translate uniformly across health-care systems. For hospitals considering transitioning from a 0/3 h to a 0/1 h pathway, implementation decisions should take broader diagnostic and organisational considerations into account. FUNDING: Swiss National Science Foundation, Swiss Heart Foundation, University Hospital Basel, University of Basel, and Foundation for Cardiovascular Research Basel.

Eur Heart J Acute Cardiovasc Care 2026-10-25 Observational Acute and critical care n = 58,668 impact 4/5

Earlier Versus Later Extracorporeal Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation in Refractory Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Nationwide Sequential Landmark Target Trial Emulation.

Sim JH, Park B, Lee H · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) may improve outcomes in refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA), but the optimal timing of initiation remains uncertain, partly because prior comparisons did not restrict controls to patients still in refractory arrest at the same decision point. METHODS: We employed target trial emulation with a sequential landmark design in the Korean nationwide OHCA registry (2016-2023). Among patients with presumed cardiac aetiology who were still receiving emergency department (ED) CPR 15 minutes after ED arrival, landmarks were set at 15, 30, and 60 minutes, each comparing ECPR initiation within the subsequent window with no ECPR during that window among patients still in refractory arrest. Treatment effects were adjusted risk differences estimated by propensity score overlap weighting with 22 covariates. RESULTS: Of 58,668 patients with refractory OHCA, 909 received ECPR. Weighted 30-day survival (ECPR vs. control) was 31.0% vs. 16.0%, 21.6% vs. 7.2%, and 10.9% vs. 3.2% at the 15-, 30-, and 60-minute landmarks; adjusted risk differences were +15.0 percentage points (95% CI 8.0 to 22.0), +14.4 (10.4 to 18.4), and +7.7 (3.7 to 11.7) (all P<.001). Favourable neurological outcomes showed a similar pattern, and in the exploratory timing-gradient analysis, the adjusted risk difference decreased with longer door-to-ECMO times but remained statistically significant through 120 minutes. CONCLUSIONS: ECPR was associated with improved survival and neurological outcomes at every landmark, including initiation beyond 60 minutes, where weighted 30-day survival with continued conventional CPR was 3.2%. Benefit was largest with early initiation but did not disappear at the commonly applied 60-minute benchmark, which may be overly restrictive for selected patients in capable systems.
