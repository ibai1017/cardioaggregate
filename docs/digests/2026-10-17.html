Intraoperative Dobutamine Plus Norepinephrine to Prevent Postoperative Acute Kidney Injury in Septic Shock: A Randomized Clinical Trial.

Chinese Perioperative Septic Shock Investigators for the Chinese Peri-Operative Organ Protection Group et al. · PubMed

Abstract

IMPORTANCE: International guidelines recommend dobutamine for management of septic shock and cardiac dysfunction with persistent hypoperfusion in adults based on very low-certainty evidence. Randomized trials are lacking on the effect of intraoperative dobutamine combined with norepinephrine vs norepinephrine on postoperative outcomes in patients with septic shock undergoing surgical source control. OBJECTIVE: To determine whether intraoperative dobutamine combined with norepinephrine reduces acute kidney injury (AKI) within 7 postoperative days in patients with septic shock undergoing surgical source control. DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This randomized, open-label clinical trial in 39 tertiary hospitals in China enrolled adults with septic shock who were undergoing surgical source control between June 2024 and August 2025. Follow-up ended on November 15, 2025. INTERVENTION: Intraoperative, intravenous infusion of dobutamine (up to 5 µg/kg/min) combined with norepinephrine vs norepinephrine alone. MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary outcome was new-onset AKI or worsening of preoperative AKI within 7 days after surgery, defined using Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria. The 11 secondary outcomes included Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score on postoperative day 1 and urine output 1 hour post intervention. RESULTS: Among 584 patients (median [IQR] age, 67.5 [55.0-76.0] years; 192 [32.9%] females), new-onset or worsening AKI within 7 postoperative days occurred in 22.8% (66/290) randomized to receive dobutamine plus norepinephrine vs 34.7% (102/294) randomized to receive norepinephrine alone (adjusted risk ratio [ARR], 0.64 [95% CI, 0.49-0.83]). No significant differences were observed in 9 of the 11 secondary outcomes. SOFA scores on postoperative day 1 (adjusted difference, -0.53 [95% CI, -0.98 to -0.07]) were lower and urine output 1 hour post intervention (adjusted difference, 0.55 [95% CI, 0.03 to 1.07] mL/kg/h) was higher in the dobutamine plus norepinephrine group. Stage 2 and 3 AKI were lower in the dobutamine plus norepinephrine group compared with the norepinephrine alone group (stage 2: 4.8% vs 9.9%; ARR, 0.48 [95% CI, 0.26-0.88]; stage 3: 7.9% vs 13.6%; ARR, 0.56 [95% CI, 0.35-0.91]). Tachycardia (heart rate >100/min) occurred more frequently in patients receiving dobutamine and norepinephrine than norepinephrine alone. CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Among adult patients with septic shock undergoing surgical source control, intraoperative dobutamine combined with norepinephrine reduced the incidence of new-onset or worsening AKI within 7 postoperative days compared with norepinephrine. TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06462313.

Early Discharge Strategy Including Subcutaneous Furosemide in Hospitalized Heart Failure.

Campbell RT et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Patients hospitalized for heart failure often require prolonged intravenous diuretic therapy, contributing to costs and risks associated with inpatient care. OBJECTIVES: We evaluated whether an early discharge strategy using subcutaneous furosemide delivered via a wearable minipump could increase days alive and out of hospital (DAOH). METHODS: In this multicenter, open-label, randomized, active-controlled trial in the United Kingdom, 172 patients hospitalized for heart failure requiring ongoing parenteral diuretics were randomly assigned to an early discharge strategy or usual care. Patients in the early discharge group received pH-neutral subcutaneous furosemide via a wearable, patient-operated minipump; postdischarge care included a review every 3 days while receiving subcutaneous furosemide. Both groups had a 2-week clinic review. The primary outcome was DAOH at 30 days after randomization. RESULTS: A total of 92 patients were assigned to early discharge and 80 to usual care. The early discharge strategy resulted in 4.0 more DAOH at 30 days (95% CI: 2.1-5.8 days; P < 0.001). Index hospitalization was shorter by a mean of 5.5 days (95% CI: 7.4-3.6 days; P < 0.001). The benefit in DAOH was sustained at 60 days (difference 4.2 days; 95% CI: 0.9-7.6 days), despite more heart failure readmissions in the early discharge group (13 [14%] patients vs 5 [6%] patients; OR: 2.45; 95% CI: 0.81-7.43). Serious adverse events occurred in 25% and 23% of patients, respectively. Device-related problems occurred with 3% of minipump placements, but none were serious. CONCLUSIONS: An early discharge strategy using a subcutaneous furosemide minipump increased DAOH compared with usual care. (Use of a Novel SUBCUTaneous Preparation of Furosemide to Facilitate Early Supported Discharge of Patients With Heart Failure [SUBCUT-HF II]; NCT05419115).

Occlusion-Focused Triage vs Very-High-Risk NSTEMI Criteria: A DIFOCCULT-3 Substudy.

Aslanger EK et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: The current ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)/NSTEMI framework may not fully identify patients with ACO who present without diagnostic STE. Although immediate invasive management is recommended for patients with very-high-risk NSTEMI features, these criteria are inconsistently applied in clinical practice. The OMI/NOMI framework has been proposed as an alternative strategy. OBJECTIVES: This study sought to compare the occlusive and nonocclusive myocardial infarction (OMI/NOMI) classification with guideline-defined very-high-risk non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) criteria for the identification of acute coronary occlusion (ACO) in patients without diagnostic STE. METHODS: This was a substudy of the multicenter, cluster-randomized DIFOCCULT-3 (Time for a Diagnostic Paradigm Shift From ST-Elevation/Non-ST-Elevation to Occlusion/Non-Occlusion Myocardial Infarction-3) trial. Consecutive adult patients without diagnostic STE on the admission electrocardiogram were included and managed according to either the conventional STEMI/NSTEMI or the OMI/NOMI approach. Very-high-risk NSTEMI was defined according to current guidelines. Outcomes included immediate invasive management (within 2 hours) and adjudicated ACO. RESULTS: Among 4,398 patients, very-high-risk NSTEMI criteria identified a small proportion of patients and were inconsistently translated into early intervention. The OMI/NOMI approach selected more patients for early invasive management compared with the STEMI/NSTEMI framework (45.4% vs 13.6%; P < 0.001). Adjudicated ACO was identified within 2 hours more frequently under the OMI/NOMI approach (35.1% vs 16.7%; P < 0.001), without reduction in angiographic yield. CONCLUSIONS: An occlusion-focused diagnostic approach was associated with substantially higher rates of immediate invasive management and earlier identification of patients with adjudicated ACO.

JACC Heart Fail 2026-10-13 RCT secondary analysis Heart failure n = 605 impact 3/5

Long-Term Safety and Procedural Performance of an Interatrial Shunt for Heart Failure According to Ejection Fraction.

Rodés-Cabau J et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Long-term device durability and phenotype-specific safety outcomes after interatrial shunting require further characterization. OBJECTIVES: This study aimed to evaluate the procedural success, device durability, and heart failure (HF) phenotype-specific safety outcomes of an interatrial shunt in the RELIEVE-HF trial. METHODS: Randomized patients were assigned 1:1 to shunt or sham control with prespecified stratification by left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) into heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) (LVEF ≤40%) and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) (LVEF >40%). Procedural performance, device durability, serious adverse events (SAEs), major adverse cardiovascular or neurological events (MACNE), major bleeding, and renal outcomes were assessed through 2 years. RESULTS: Among 605 enrolled patients (508 randomized; 97 roll-in), shunt implantation was attempted in 370 and was successful in 369 (99.7%). Among implanted patients with follow-up (n = 348), no device- or procedure-related MACNE occurred through 2 years (0%; 95% CI: 0%-0.9%), and shunt patency was 99.4% (95% CI: 97.9%-99.9%). Prespecified LVEF-stratified analyses demonstrated divergent safety profiles. In HFrEF, shunt therapy was not associated with excess all-cause MACNE, major bleeding, or renal decline and was associated with lower SAE burden. In HFpEF, shunt therapy was associated with higher all-cause MACNE, greater SAE burden, increased major bleeding, and greater renal decline. CONCLUSIONS: Interatrial shunt implantation was mechanically reliable and durable through 2 years. Safety outcomes differed by HF phenotype, with no evidence of excess clinical safety events in HFrEF but increased adverse events in HFpEF. These findings support phenotype-specific evaluation of interatrial shunt therapy. (REducing Lung congestion symptoms using the v-wavE shunt in adVancEd Heart Failure [RELIEVE-HF]; NCT03499236).

Eur J Prev Cardiol 2026-10-16 RCT Hypertension n = 150 impact 2/5

Effect of Colchicine on Arterial Stiffness in Patients With Hypertension: The COlchicine HypERtENsion Trial (COHERENT).

Langhoff AF et al. · PubMed

Abstract

AIM: This trial investigated whether colchicine, an anti-inflammatory treatment, affects cardiovascular structure and function, assessed by arterial stiffness, blood pressure and biochemistry, compared with placebo. METHODS: COHERENT was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of the effects of colchicine on arterial stiffness in adults with pharmacologically treated hypertension without cardiovascular disease. Eligible patients were randomized 1:1 to colchicine 0.5mg daily or placebo for six months. Assessment of arterial stiffness, echocardiography, and biomarker measurement were performed at baseline and six months. The primary endpoint was change in carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity (cfPWV). Secondary endpoints included change in blood pressure (BP), left ventricular mass index, as well as change in cardiac and inflammatory biomarkers. RESULTS: Of 183 screened, 150 were randomized (75 per group). Mean age was 64.5 (±8.0) years, 53 (35.3%) were female, and mean systolic blood pressure was 137.6 (±15.6) mmHg. At six months, cfPWV did not differ between groups (between-group difference in ΔcfPWV -0.1m/s, 95% CI: -0.5 to 0.3m/s, p=0.53). Office systolic BP decreased significantly more in the placebo group than in the colchicine group (between-group difference in Δoffice systolic BP 5.7 mmHg, 95% CI: 1.0 to 10.5 mmHg, p=0.018. hsCRP decreased significantly in the colchicine group compared with the placebo group (between-group difference in % change in hsCRP -39.8%, 95% CI: -59.1 to -11.4%, p=0.010). Left ventricular mass index or cardiac biomarkers did not differ between groups. CONCLUSIONS: Six months of colchicine did not affect arterial stiffness in pharmacologically treated hypertension without cardiovascular disease. Colchicine significantly decreased hsCRP.

Intracoronary imaging to assess atherosclerosis and to guide percutaneous coronary intervention.

Vergallo R et al. · PubMed

Abstract

Intracoronary imaging has become an important tool for the in vivo assessment of coronary atherosclerosis and for guiding percutaneous coronary intervention. By enabling high-resolution visualization of coronary anatomy, these modalities allow for more accurate lesion characterization, precise stent sizing, and optimized procedural strategies. In recent years, multiple randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that imaging-guided percutaneous coronary intervention reduces major adverse cardiac events (including cardiac death, myocardial infarction, and repeat revascularization), particularly in complex clinical and anatomical settings. This state-of-the-art review synthesizes contemporary evidence supporting the use of intravascular ultrasound and optical coherence tomography to guide percutaneous coronary intervention and improve clinical outcomes, drawing from recent trial data and current guideline recommendations. Special emphasis is placed on complex lesion subsets, such as left main coronary artery disease and other complex bifurcation lesions, calcified lesions, long lesions, and scenarios involving patients at heightened risk, such as those with acute coronary syndrome. Finally, this review considers the strengths and limitations of intracoronary imaging, as well as unresolved questions and priority areas for future research aimed at further improving patient outcomes.

Eur Heart J 2026-10-14 Other Acute and critical care impact 3/5

Temporary mechanical circulatory support in cardiogenic shock.

Chieffo A et al. · PubMed

Abstract

Cardiogenic shock (CS) remains a heterogeneous syndrome associated with persistently high mortality despite advances in temporary mechanical circulatory support. One recent randomized controlled trial suggested a mortality benefit of a percutaneous microaxial flow pump in a cohort of patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction complicated by CS, whereas routine veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation in populations with acute myocardial infarction complicated by CS has not improved survival and is associated with higher complication rates. These findings underscore the importance of phenotype-driven patient selection, incorporating the Society for Cardiovascular Angiography and Interventions (SCAI) and Shock Academic Research Consortium (SHARC) classifications to guide device selection, timing, escalation, and de-escalation. This clinical consensus statement from the European Association of Percutaneous Cardiovascular Interventions (EAPCI) and the Association for Acute CardioVascular Care (ACVC) of the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) updates the 2020 expert consensus document and provides updated practical guidance based on the recent evidence on the use of temporary mechanical circulatory support in acute myocardial infarction complicated by CS. Emphasis is placed on patient/device selection according to recent evidence, multidisciplinary shock teams, regionalized hub-and-spoke systems, structured protocols for escalation and futility, and the integration of contemporary trial data and best practices to optimize outcomes in this high-risk population.

The Impact of Statin Therapy on Postoperative Short- and Long-term Mortality and Morbidity After Non-Cardiac Surgery.

Bell M et al. · PubMed

Abstract

AIMS: Statins' anti-inflammatory and endothelial-stabilising effects suggest perioperative value, but trials of de novo initiation were discredited following research misconduct. Whether established preoperative statin therapy lowers postoperative mortality dose-dependently remains unclear. METHODS: We studied 56,362 patients aged ≥45 years undergoing non-cardiac surgery at two university hospitals (2016-2023). Preoperative statin exposure was assessed as status (any vs none), intensity (high vs low/intermediate), and regimen (high vs low/intermediate vs none). Primary outcomes were 30- and 365-day mortality. Inverse probability of treatment weighting, using random forest-estimated propensity scores targeting the average treatment effect in the treated, was applied to balance measured confounders. Associations were estimated with weighted Cox regression. RESULTS: Overall, 372 patients (0.6%) died within 30 days and 3,735 (6.6%) within 365 days. After weighting, all covariates were balanced. Statin therapy showed a directionally consistent but non-significant association with lower 30-day mortality (HR 0.78; 95% CI 0.57-1.07), and was associated with lower 365-day mortality (HR 0.81; 95% CI 0.73-0.90; ARD -1.2 percentage points; number needed to treat 83). A dose-response relationship was evident: versus no statin, high-intensity therapy showed a 29% lower hazard (HR 0.71; 95% CI 0.60-0.83; ARD -2.3 points) and low/intermediate-intensity a 16% lower hazard (HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.74-0.96). Findings were consistent across sensitivity analyses. CONCLUSIONS: Among adults undergoing non-cardiac surgery, established preoperative statin therapy was associated with dose-dependent lower 365-day mortality. These findings suggest perioperative statin continuation as a preventive strategy and warrant confirmation in a randomised trial.

EuroIntervention 2026-10-01 Other Interventional impact 1/5

Combined ischaemia and vulnerable plaque percutaneous coronary intervention to reduce cardiovascular events: design and rationale of the COMBINE-INTERVENE Trial.

Kedhi E et al. · PubMed

Abstract

Fractional flow reserve (FFR) assessment represents the most widely used revascularisation strategy of the last two decades. However, many FFR (+) lesions, particularly those in the grey zone, can be safely treated medically, while other lesions carrying vulnerable plaque (VP) features can cause future events despite the absence of ischaemia. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) can identify lesions with VP morphology. The objective of this study is to determine whether a combined revascularisation strategy - targeting only severely ischaemic lesions (FFR ≤0.75) and extending treatment to OCT-assessed VP, even in the absence of ischaemia (FFR>0.80) - could reduce future adverse events compared with the current revascularisation practice based exclusively on the presence of ischaemia (FFR ≤0.80). The COMBINE-INTERVENE Trial is a global, prospective, randomised, single-blinded, sham-controlled, superiority trial. A total of 1,223 all-comer patients with multivessel disease (≥2 lesions with a diameter of stenosis ≥50%) will be randomised 1:1 to revascularisation if the FFR is ≤0.75 or a VP is present (experimental arm) versus FFR ≤0.80 in the comparative arm. The primary endpoint is a composite of cardiac death, any myocardial infarction, or any clinically driven revascularisation within 24 months. The trial has already completed enrolment in 50 centres worldwide. COMBINE-INTERVENE is the first trial evaluating the impact of a revascularisation strategy that targets lesions that are either severely ischaemic or carry an OCT-assessed VP versus revascularisation based on ischaemia alone. The trial is powered for superiority. (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05333068).

JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026-10-12 Observational Electrophysiology n = 932 impact 3/5

Conduction System Versus Right Ventricular Pacing in Patients Undergoing TAVR: A Multicenter Propensity-Matched Study.

Diez-Delhoyo F et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Pacemaker implantation after transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) is common and has been associated with adverse clinical outcomes. Cardiac physiological pacing (CPP) may preserve ventricular synchrony and reduce heart failure (HF) events compared with right ventricular pacing (RVP). OBJECTIVES: This study sought to compare long-term clinical outcomes of CPP vs RVP in patients requiring permanent pacing after TAVR. METHODS: Registro de Estimulación Fisiológica-TAVR is a retrospective, multicenter nationwide registry including patients undergoing TAVR and pacemaker implantation within 30 days. Propensity score matching (1:1) was performed using baseline clinical and echocardiographic variables, yielding 466 matched pairs. The primary endpoint was the composite of cardiovascular death or HF hospitalization. Cox proportional hazards models were used for time-to-event analyses. Median follow-up was 24 months. RESULTS: In the propensity score-matched cohort, CPP was associated with a lower incidence of the primary endpoint than RVP (9.7% vs 17.6%; HR: 0.57; 95% CI: 0.39-0.83; P = 0.003). This difference was driven by fewer HF hospitalizations (7.9% vs 13.9%; HR: 0.57; 95% CI: 0.37-0.86; P = 0.008). No significant differences were observed in cardiovascular death or all-cause mortality between groups. CONCLUSIONS: In this multicenter propensity score-matched study, CPP after TAVR was associated with a lower risk of the primary endpoint, driven by fewer HF hospitalizations. These findings support the need for further randomized clinical trials to evaluate the role of CPP in post-TAVR patients requiring ventricular pacing.
