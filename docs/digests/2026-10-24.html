Circulation 2026-10-19 RCT Acute and critical care n = 59,980 impact 4/5

ICare-FASTER (Improving Care by Faster Risk Stratification Through Use of High-Sensitivity Point-of-Care Troponin in Patients Presenting With Possible Acute Coronary Syndrome in the Emergency Department): A Stepped-Wedge Cluster-Randomized Trial.

Than M et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Point-of-care high-sensitivity cTn (cardiac troponin; hs-cTn) testing has the potential to expedite decision-making and reduce emergency department (ED) length of stay for patients presenting with possible myocardial infarction by ensuring that results are consistently available when sought by clinicians. We assessed the real-life effectiveness and safety of implementing point-of-care hs-cTn testing in the ED. METHODS: We conducted a pragmatic, stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial. The control arm was usual care with an accelerated diagnostic pathway using a single-sample rule-out step with a central laboratory hs-cTn assay. The intervention arm used the same pathway with a point-of-care hs-cTnI assay. The primary effectiveness outcome was ED length of stay assessed using a generalized linear mixed model. The safety outcome was myocardial infarction or cardiac death within 30 days. RESULTS: Six sites participated, with 59 980 ED presentations (44 747 individuals; mean 61±19 years of age; 49.5% female) from February 2023 through January 2025; 31 392 presentations were during the intervention arm. After adjustment for covariates associated with length of stay, the intervention reduced length of stay by 13% (95% CI, 9%-16%; P<0.001), corresponding to a reduction of 47 minutes (95% CI, 33-61 minutes) from a mean length of stay in the control arm of 376 minutes. The 30-day myocardial infarction or cardiac death rate was similar in the control and intervention arms (0.39% and 0.39%, respectively; P=0.54). CONCLUSIONS: Implementation of whole-blood hs-cTnI testing at the point of care into an accelerated diagnostic pathway was safe and reduced length of stay in the ED compared with laboratory testing. REGISTRATION: URL: https://www.anzctr.org.au; Unique identifier: ACTRN12619001189112.

EuroIntervention 2026-10-19 Meta-analysis Structural and valve n = 1,476 impact 4/5

Coronary artery disease management in patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials.

Bujak K et al. · PubMed

Abstract

BACKGROUND: The optimal management of coronary artery disease in patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) remains uncertain. AIMS: We aimed to compare clinical outcomes between routine coronary revascularisation and conservative management in patients undergoing TAVI and to assess the relative effectiveness of individual revascularisation strategies by performing a meta-analysis of randomised trials. METHODS: We conducted a pairwise meta-analysis comparing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with conservative treatment and a network meta-analysis comparing the relative effectiveness of individual strategies, including angiography-guided and physiology-guided PCI. The primary outcome was a study-defined composite endpoint of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Secondary endpoints included the individual outcomes of all-cause death, cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, stroke, unplanned revascularisation, and major bleeding. RESULTS: Five trials (N=1,644 patients) were included in the systematic review, and four were included in the primary quantitative synthesis (N=1,476). Compared with conservative treatment, routine PCI was associated with lower risks of MACE (risk ratio [RR] 0.80, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.65-0.98) and unplanned revascularisation (RR 0.30, 95% CI: 0.11-0.83) and an increased risk of major bleeding (RR 1.57, 95% CI: 1.07-2.32). In the network meta-analysis, physiology-guided PCI significantly reduced MACE, stroke, and unplanned revascularisation compared with conservative treatment and ranked highest for all ischaemic outcomes among revascularisation strategies. CONCLUSIONS: Among patients referred for TAVI, routine revascularisation reduces the risk of ischaemic outcomes compared with conservative treatment. In this setting, physiology-guided PCI may be associated with the most favourable efficacy profile across available strategies. (PROSPERO: CRD420261330194).

Large observational studies

JAMA Cardiol 2026-10-23 Observational Prevention and lipids n = 12,847 impact 4/5

Advanced Subclinical Atherosclerosis in Young Individuals and Risk of Mortality Over 30 Years: The Coronary Artery Calcium Consortium.

Choi S et al. · PubMed

Abstract

IMPORTANCE: Coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring is a well-established tool for predicting cardiovascular risk, but its long-term prognostic value in young adults remains unclear. As early preventive intervention may yield substantial lifetime benefit, understanding long-term implications of elevated CAC in young adults is important for informing prevention strategies. OBJECTIVE: To investigate the long-term risk of all-cause, cardiovascular disease (CVD), and coronary heart disease (CHD) mortality associated with premature advanced subclinical atherosclerosis. DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This cohort study used the CAC Consortium dataset, a retrospectively assembled cohort of referred individuals with clinically indicated CAC scans. Using multivariable regression models for time-to-event analysis, the association of CAC with all-cause, CVD, and CHD mortality was assessed, adjusting for age, sex, and traditional atherosclerotic CVD risk factors. Cumulative incidence was derived for these outcomes in 5-year increments up to 30 years using a mixed approach involving the Kaplan-Meier method and Weibull modeling. The proportion of deaths attributable to CVD and CHD across CAC groups was evaluated. Four institutions across the US provided patient-level data to assemble the CAC Consortium. Included in the analysis were adults aged 30 to 45 years from the CAC Consortium. They were followed up for a median of 22.6 years. Study data were analyzed from October 2025 to September 2026. EXPOSURE: Elevated CAC in adults aged 30 to 45 years, specifically CAC score greater than 90%. MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Participants were followed up for underlying cause of mortality. RESULTS: Among 12 847 participants, the mean (SD) age was 40.5 (3.6) years, and 9922 (77.2%) were male; 9262 (72.1%) had CAC score of 0, 1626 (12.7%) had a CAC score between 75% to 90%, and 1703 (13.3%) had a CAC score greater than 90%. In total, there were 624 deaths (149 CVD and 81 CHD). Multivariable adjusted hazard ratios for CAC score greater than 90% vs CAC score of 0 were 1.87 (95% CI, 1.54-2.27), 2.60 (95% CI, 1.77-3.82), and 3.71 (95% CI, 2.24-6.16), for all-cause, CVD, and CHD mortality, respectively. Over 30 years, the group with a CAC score greater than 90% had significantly higher modeled risk of all-cause (12.9% vs 7.5%), CVD (4.5% vs 1.5%), and CHD (3.0% vs 0.6%) mortality, compared with the group with a CAC score of 0. CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Results of this cohort study suggest that young adults with a CAC score greater than 90% face significantly higher long-term risk of all-cause, CVD, and CHD mortality, suggesting potential implications for guiding the intensification of preventive intervention in this high-risk phenotype.
